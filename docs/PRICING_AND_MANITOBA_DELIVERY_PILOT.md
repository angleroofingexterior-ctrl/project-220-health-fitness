# Project 220 Pricing and Manitoba Delivery Pilot

## Launch strategy

Project 220 launches with a Winnipeg operational pilot and Manitoba-wide service availability where an approved shopper/driver and participating retailer can fulfill the order. Same-day service is not guaranteed outside Winnipeg. Rural and northern orders may use scheduled delivery days, consolidated routes, pickup hubs, or approved third-party delivery partners.

## Recommended subscription pricing (CAD)

### Free
- Personalized starter assessment
- Basic workout and meal plan
- Limited logging
- One 3-day meal plan and grocery list per month
- Local-only privacy mode

### Project 220 Plus
- $9.99/month or $79.99/year
- Adaptive workout, nutrition, supplement-education, grocery and schedule plans
- Full workout and nutrition logs
- Proper-form instructions
- 7-day meal planning
- Pantry inventory and manual grocery comparison

### Project 220 Complete
- $14.99/month or $119.99/year
- Everything in Plus
- Full 28–31-day cookbook
- Pantry/fridge/cupboard photo inventory with user confirmation
- Automatic meal substitutions and grocery recalculation
- Advanced progress tracking, boxing/cardio modules and privacy-safe encrypted sync when implemented
- Preferred access to Project 220 delivery scheduling

### Affordability plan
- $4.99/month for verified low-income users, students, seniors, disability-benefit recipients and approved community-program referrals
- Same core planning features as Plus
- No advertising and no sale of personal data

Subscriptions do not include groceries, delivery charges or optional tips.

## Delivery pricing recommendation

All charges must be shown before confirmation. No hidden fees.

### Zone 1: Winnipeg
- Standard scheduled delivery: $8.99
- Large order over 40 unique items: +$3.00
- Priority delivery when available: +$5.00
- Multi-store order: +$5.00 for each additional store

### Zone 2: Capital Region / nearby communities
- Base fee: $12.99
- Distance charge after the included radius: $0.65/km for the additional one-way distance

### Zone 3: Southern and Central Manitoba
- Base fee: $19.99
- Distance charge: $0.65/km beyond the local service radius
- Prefer scheduled route days and order batching to lower costs

### Zone 4: Northern and remote Manitoba
- Quoted before checkout using actual route, carrier or community-hub costs
- Display lower-cost pickup, scheduled route and partner-delivery alternatives
- Never promise same-day service when it cannot be safely fulfilled

## Delivery membership add-on

After pilot economics are verified:
- $7.99/month or $69/year
- Reduced standard delivery fees on eligible orders
- Minimum grocery subtotal of $50
- Long-distance, priority, multi-store and remote-route charges still apply

## Driver compensation guardrails

- Driver pay must be calculated separately from tips.
- Suggested minimum per completed Winnipeg shop-and-deliver order: $12 base plus item, time and distance adjustments.
- Mileage component should be reviewed regularly against fuel and vehicle costs.
- Tips cannot be used to reduce guaranteed driver compensation.

## Optional tipping

- Tip is always optional.
- Checkout choices: No tip, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, or custom amount.
- Default must not be preselected in a manipulative way.
- 100% of the tip goes to the assigned shopper/driver.
- Customers may increase a tip after delivery and may report service problems through a fair dispute process.

## Cost-comparison rules

The cheapest recommendation must compare the final payable basket, not just shelf price. Include:
- Required quantities and package sizes
- Unit price
- Sales and loyalty prices available without selling customer data
- Store-brand alternatives
- Delivery, service, distance, priority and multi-store fees
- Applicable taxes, deposits and bag fees
- Nutritional equivalence, allergies and dietary restrictions
- Expected waste, leftovers and pantry inventory

## Winnipeg pilot

Start with a controlled Winnipeg pilot before scaling operational density across Manitoba.

Pilot requirements:
- 25–100 invited households
- 5–15 screened drivers/shoppers
- Limited operating hours and delivery zones
- Walmart, Superstore, No Frills and other stores only where lawful access and purchasing procedures are available
- Manual price verification and receipt reconciliation before retailer API approval
- Customer-driver in-app messaging and substitution approvals
- Item-by-item driver checklist
- Proof of purchase and proof of delivery
- Privacy separation between fitness data and delivery data
- Service quality, driver earnings, late-delivery, substitution, refund and basket-savings metrics

## Province-wide rollout

Manitoba may be presented as the launch province, but checkout must only accept an order when:
- A driver, route or approved partner is available
- A participating store can fulfill the basket
- The customer sees the complete price and realistic delivery window

Use scheduled community routes and pickup hubs for low-density regions rather than charging unsustainable individual-trip prices.

## Global design

- Store currency, taxes, units, language, retailer rules and delivery regulations by region
- Add countries and regions only after payments, privacy, insurance, worker classification and retail access are validated
- Keep Instacart or another approved aggregator as a fallback where direct Project 220 fulfillment is unavailable

## Market reference summary

Pricing should remain competitive with established fitness apps while reflecting Project 220's broader workout, nutrition, cookbook, grocery and privacy features. Current public references reviewed in July 2026 included MyFitnessPal Canadian in-app prices, MacroFactor's USD monthly and annual plans, Fitbod's USD monthly and annual plans, Walmart Canada's Delivery Pass, and Instacart Canada's variable delivery/service-fee structure.

## Required owner actions

1. Approve the subscription tiers and delivery pricing for pilot testing.
2. Register the delivery business activities and obtain Manitoba legal/accounting advice.
3. Obtain commercial general liability, cyber/privacy, cargo/goods and delivery-driver insurance.
4. Select payment processing and driver-payout providers.
5. Establish driver screening, contracts, safety, reimbursement and incident procedures.
6. Apply for official retailer/API/affiliate relationships.
7. Recruit pilot customers and drivers.
8. Approve refund, substitution, tipping, nondiscrimination and accessibility policies.
9. Run a four-to-eight-week Winnipeg pilot before locking final pricing.
